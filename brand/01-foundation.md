# 01 · Foundation

What Mwavuli stands for and how it sounds. Everything visual in the other files serves this file. Rules carry a strength
word and a source tag; see the [README](README.md) for the scale and the tags.

## Purpose and character

### F1 · Purpose and promise

*Strength: Always · Source: Board*

**Rules**

- **Always** state the purpose in the board's words: "Professional property management for people and businesses in
  Uganda." _(Board)_
- **Always** speak to both audiences the purpose names: people (owners and tenants) and businesses (commercial owners
  and occupiers). _(Board)_
- **Prefer** the outcome before the mechanism: say what the owner or tenant gets ("Better Living", "Our
  Responsibility"), then how Mwavuli delivers it. _(Card)_
- **Avoid** promises the service list does not back. The services on the business card are the proof points: guaranteed
  rental income on the 1st, tenant management and sourcing, construction and repairs, electrical and plumbing,
  utilities, garbage disposal. _(Proposed)_

**Why** A property manager sells trust. A purpose that is short, local and specific is easier to repeat than a mission
statement, and a promise that only claims what the services deliver cannot be caught out.

**Example** Site headline: "Managed Properties. Better Living." Proof line beneath it: "Rent paid to you on the 1st,
every month."

**Bend when** A campaign targets one audience only (for example commercial tenants): lead with that audience, keep the
purpose statement in the footer.

**Prior art** GOV.UK's design principles open with one plain sentence ("Start with user needs") and put the proof after
it; the same order is used here.

### F2 · Essence pillars

*Strength: Always · Source: Board*

**Rules**

- **Always** treat the four pillars as the complete brand essence. Do not add a fifth in any one deliverable. _(Board)_
- **Always** use the pillar names and one-line descriptions as written below. _(Board)_
- **Default** to one lead pillar per page, poster or campaign; the others appear as supporting proof. _(Proposed)_
- **Always** back a pillar claim with a service fact, not an adjective. _(Proposed)_

| Pillar             | Description                        | Icon      | Proof in the services       |
|--------------------|------------------------------------|-----------|-----------------------------|
| Trusted            | Secure, compliant, and dependable. | trusted   | Guaranteed rent; compliance |
| People-Centric     | Great spaces, happy tenants.       | tenants   | Tenant management, sourcing |
| Organized          | Efficient, transparent management. | organized | Statements and reports      |
| Invested in Growth | Maximizing property value.         | growth    | Repairs that protect value  |

**Why** Four pillars fit on one slide and one screen, and each maps to something a client can check.

**Example** A tenant page leads with People-Centric: "Great spaces, happy tenants." The proof is a repair-request flow
with a promised response time, not the word "caring".

**Bend when** A regulator-facing or investor document may lead with Trusted and Organized together; keep the other two
as footnotes.

**Prior art** Brand pillars with one-line proofs are standard in brand books; the discipline here is the same as
GOV.UK's "start with user needs": every claim is tied to something the user can verify.

### F3 · Audience and positioning

*Strength: Default · Source: Proposed*

**Rules**

- **Default** to three audiences: property owners and investors, residential tenants, and commercial occupiers.
  _(Proposed)_
- **Default** to this positioning line unless the owner replaces it: "For property owners and tenants in Uganda, Mwavuli
  is the property manager that keeps the rent coming, the units occupied and the buildings in good repair, with clear
  records." _(Proposed)_
- **Always** write the positioning line from facts in the service list, and update it when the service list changes.
  _(Proposed)_

**Why** The board gives a purpose and essence but no audience definition. These three groups are what the app mock and
the service list imply (Residential and Commercial units; owners paying through tenants' rent).

**Example** Owner page: rent, statements, upkeep. Tenant page: payments, repairs, bookings. Commercial page: leases,
compliance, reports.

**Bend when** Research shows a different primary audience: replace this concern (decision D3).

**Prior art** Positioning statements of the "for [who], [brand] is the [category] that [benefit]" form (Geoffrey Moore,
*Crossing the Chasm*).

### F4 · Personality

*Strength: Always · Source: Board*

**Rules**

- **Always** apply all five principles from the board's Visual Style list; none is optional. _(Board)_
- **Always** resolve a conflict between principles in this order: Clean and uncluttered, Modern and professional, Warm
  and welcoming, Property and people focused, Consistent. _(Proposed)_

| Principle                              | What it means in practice                                          |
|----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Modern and professional                | Geometric sans, flat color, precise alignment, no dated ornament   |
| Clean and uncluttered                  | Generous white space, three colors at most, one idea per block     |
| Warm and welcoming                     | Cream grounds, gold accents, daylight photography, friendly copy   |
| Property and people focused            | Buildings and residents in imagery, roof forms, people icons       |
| Consistent across digital and physical | The same tokens, icons and lockups on screen, card, sign and shirt |

**Why** Principles give reviewers something concrete to point at. The order resolves the usual clash between warmth and
minimalism in favor of calm.

**Example** A promotional banner that wants a sixth color and a drop shadow fails Clean and uncluttered, whatever else
it does well.

**Bend when** A physical application needs texture (embossing on a polo, dimensional letters): the form may be richer,
the palette and geometry may not.

**Prior art** GOV.UK's design principles and Material Design's principles are short lists a reviewer can point at; the
explicit order is added here so conflicts can be settled.

## Voice

### F5 · Voice and tone

*Strength: Default · Source: Card, Proposed*

**Rules**

- **Default** to a plain, specific, warm, assured voice. Short sentences, active verbs, concrete numbers and dates.
  _(Proposed)_
- **Always** write to the reader as "you" and "your property". _(Card)_
- **Avoid** hype, jargon, exclamation marks and superlatives. _(Proposed)_
- **Prefer** facts over adjectives: "Rent on the 1st" beats "reliable payments". _(Card)_
- **Default** to sentence case in interface text and Title Case in taglines and headlines. _(Proposed)_

**Why** The audience includes first-time tenants and owners who are not property professionals. Plain words read as
honest, and honesty is the brand's first pillar.

**Example** Do: "Your repair request is booked for Tuesday. A technician will call you the day before." Don't: "We're
thrilled to action your maintenance ticket!"

**Bend when** Legal or regulatory notices need fixed wording; keep the layout and typography on brand.

**Prior art** Plain-language guidance in the GOV.UK content style guide and the Mailchimp Content Style Guide.

### F6 · Taglines and copy patterns

*Strength: Prefer · Source: Board, Card*

**Rules**

- **Prefer** two-beat taglines: two short declaratives, each ending with a full stop, in Title Case. _(Card, Board)_
- **Always** keep both beats together as one unit; never split them across layouts. _(Proposed)_
- **Default** to white for the first beat and gold for the second on green grounds; green and gold on cream grounds.
  _(Card)_
- **Prefer** a short gold rule under a headline when a block needs an anchor. _(Board)_

| Line                               | Use                                       | Source |
|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|--------|
| Your Property. Our Responsibility. | Primary tagline: card back, signage, site | Card   |
| Managed Properties. Better Living. | Campaign headline: hero imagery, social   | Board  |

**Why** Two beats give a rhythm that is easy to remember, and the full stops make each beat a complete claim.

**Example** "Your Property. Our Responsibility." in 11 pt Montserrat on the card back: "Your Property." white, "Our
Responsibility." gold.

**Bend when** Space allows only one beat (a favicon-sized banner): use the second beat alone, never the first.

**Prior art** Parallel two-beat lines are a standard copywriting form; the typography follows the approved business card
back.

### F7 · Naming and descriptor

*Strength: Always · Source: Board, Card*

**Rules**

- **Always** write the name as "Mwavuli" with one capital. Write the full name as "Mwavuli Property Management".
  _(Board)_
- **Always** set "PROPERTY MANAGEMENT" in tracked capitals only inside the logo lockups. Elsewhere write it in sentence
  or title case. _(Board, Card)_
- **Never** write MWAVULI or mwavuli in running text. Lowercase is allowed in domain names and email addresses only.
  _(Proposed)_
- **Consider** explaining the name where space allows: "Mwavuli" is Swahili for "umbrella", which fits the shelter idea
  in the roof motif. The board does not state this; confirm before using it (decision D2). _(Proposed)_

**Why** One written form keeps search, signage and legal documents aligned. The descriptor in capitals is a logo device,
not a house style for text.

**Example** "Mwavuli Property Management manages 30 units." Not "MWAVULI Property Management manages 30 units."

**Bend when** A contract or registry requires the registered company name in capitals: follow the registry.

**Prior art** A single declared name form is the first section of most brand style guides.

### F8 · Local formats

*Strength: Default · Source: Proposed*

**Rules**

- **Default** to English first. Add other languages only as complete, reviewed translations. _(Proposed)_
- **Always** write money as the currency code, a space and the amount with comma thousands separators: "UGX 1,200,000".
  _(Proposed)_
- **Default** to dates as day, abbreviated month, year: "9 Nov 2026". _(Proposed)_
- **Default** to phone numbers in international form on screens, "+256 7XX XXX XXX", and allow the local form "07XX XXX
  XXX" on print where space is tight, as on the business card. _(Proposed, Card)_
- **Default** to the 12-hour clock with am and pm ("10:30 am") in consumer-facing text, the 24-hour clock in logs and
  exports, and metric units with "m²". _(Proposed)_

**Why** Rent, dates and phone numbers are the most-read data in the product. One form prevents misreads between owners,
tenants and staff.

**Example** "Rent due 1 Dec 2026: UGX 1,200,000. Questions? Call +256 7XX XXX XXX."

**Bend when** An integration (payments provider, SMS gateway) dictates a format: convert at the boundary and keep the
display form.

**Prior art** The Unicode CLDR conventions for number and date formatting; ISO 4217 currency codes; E.164 for phone
numbers.
