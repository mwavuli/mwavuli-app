# 07 · Motif and imagery

The roof motif, the corner and pattern devices built from it, and the rules for photography and text over images.

## Motif

### M1 · Roof motif

*Strength: Always · Source: Board*

**Rules**

- **Always** build brand shapes from the roof form: two straight edges meeting at a peak, at the logo's slope of about
  43 degrees. _(Board, Card)_
- **Always** keep it simple: flat color, straight edges, no ornament, no perspective. _(Board)_
- **Default** to reading two roofs side by side as the letter "M" for Mwavuli, and a roof over a space as shelter.
  _(Board)_
- **Never** use the roof form to decorate unrelated content or as a bullet (L9). _(Proposed)_

**Why** The board states the idea itself: "A simple, repeatable roof form that conveys property, shelter, and the letter
'M' for Mwavuli." One shape does three jobs and is easy to reproduce in any material.

**Example** The logo mark; a row of outlined roofs as a footer pattern; a large green roof band cut off by the edge of a
banner.

**Bend when** Never for the slope. A flat-topped block device (such as the card's halftone squares) is allowed as a
corner treatment (M3) because it echoes the roof's flat color and angle discipline.

**Prior art** A single simple shape repeated at scale is the basis of many identity systems.

### M2 · Tone-on-tone pattern

*Strength: Prefer · Source: Board*

**Rules**

- **Prefer** a staggered pattern of outlined roofs, each with a small window, in a tone just darker than Mist Cream, as
  in the board's pattern panel. _(Board)_
- **Default** to `#EAEBE0` (Mist Cream with 5 percent Green mixed in) for the pattern on cream, 1.08 to 1 against the
  ground. _(Proposed)_
- **Always** keep it behind large, quiet areas (hero backgrounds, footers, empty states) and never behind body text or
  small print. _(Proposed)_
- **Avoid** the pattern on photographs, on Green, and in print below 5 percent tint. _(Proposed)_

**Why** The pattern gives cream surfaces some character without raising contrast. At that ratio it is texture, not
content.

**Example** Site footer: cream ground, pattern rows of roofs 64 px wide, offset by half a roof on alternate rows.

**Bend when** A sign or wall graphic needs the pattern larger: scale it, keep the tone.

**Prior art** Tone-on-tone patterns as background texture in identity systems; the tint limit follows the common 5
percent minimum for reliable reproduction on digital presses.

### M3 · Corner motif

*Strength: Default · Source: Card, Board*

**Rules**

- **Default** to the halftone fade for small print formats: flat squares that shrink away from a corner, green on the
  inside shifting to gold on the outside, running off the trim. _(Card)_
- **Prefer** large angular roof bands (green with a gold band at the roof slope) for large formats and screen heroes, as
  the board's motif panel and card mock show. _(Board)_
- **Always** keep corner motifs flat (no gradients, shadows or transparency), clear of text by at least 3 mm in print,
  and cropped by the trim or viewport edge. _(Card)_
- **Always** keep the smallest square at 0.9 mm or more in print (the card's smallest is 0.9 mm). _(Card)_
- **Avoid** using gold in more than about a fifth of the motif's area. _(Proposed)_

**Why** The halftone fade was chosen by the owner from 24 corner options for the business card front. The angular bands
are the board's own motif and suit larger surfaces.

**Example** Card front, bottom right: 21 squares in six diagonal steps from 3.4 mm at the corner to 0.9 mm at the edge
of the fade, on a 3.9 mm pitch; the outer two steps are gold, the rest green.

**Bend when** A format is too small for squares of 0.9 mm (a 60 by 30 mm label): use one solid roof band instead.

**Prior art** Halftone and pixel-fade devices in contemporary identities; corner motifs that bleed off the trim are
standard in card and stationery design.

### M4 · Gold rule

*Strength: Prefer · Source: Board, Card*

**Rules**

- **Prefer** one short gold rule to anchor a headline or block, left-aligned with the text. _(Board)_
- **Default** to a rule 48 to 96 px wide and 4 px thick on screen; on the card the title rule is 9.02 by 0.63 mm.
  _(Card, Proposed)_
- **Avoid** more than one gold rule in a view, and never use one as an underline for links. _(Proposed)_

**Why** The board's hero line and purpose block each end in a short gold bar; the card's job title does the same. It is
the brand's quiet underline.

**Example** "Managed Properties. Better Living." with a 64 by 4 px gold rule 16 px below.

**Bend when** Contrast or space requires it: use a Green rule on gold or a white rule on photographs.

**Prior art** A short accent rule under a heading is a long-standing editorial device.

## Imagery

### M5 · Photography

*Strength: Default · Source: Board*

**Rules**

- **Default** to bright, natural daylight, warm tones and clear skies, as in the board's hero photograph of a balconied
  mid-rise with greenery. _(Board)_
- **Prefer** real properties Mwavuli manages, shot at eye level or slightly below, with people living and working in
  them where consent allows. _(Proposed)_
- **Avoid** generic overseas stock (glass skyscrapers, snow, suburban lawns), heavy filters and dark or night scenes.
  _(Proposed)_
- **Always** record the source and license of every image used in a repository file or asset log (decision D14).
  _(Proposed)_

**Why** The first pillar is trust, and local, honest imagery earns it. The board's photograph shows the feel: modern,
clean and sunlit.

**Example** Hero: a managed apartment block at golden hour with the reversed lockup on a green band across the lower
third.

**Bend when** A real photograph is not yet available: use licensed stock that fits these rules, and replace it.

**Prior art** Authentic local photography over stock is standard guidance in brand books for service businesses.

### M6 · Text over images

*Strength: Always · Source: Proposed*

**Rules**

- **Always** put text over a photograph on a solid green band or a Green overlay at 60 to 80 percent opacity.
  _(Proposed)_
- **Always** set headlines over images in white Montserrat 700 or heavier, at 24 px or larger, with a gold rule below.
  _(Board, Proposed)_
- **Never** use a gradient fade or a drop shadow to rescue legibility. _(Proposed)_
- **Always** check the contrast of the text against the average color behind it (C3). _(Proposed)_

**Why** The board's hero sets white text straight onto the photograph, which only reads where the image is dark enough.
A band keeps the board's look and makes it readable everywhere.

**Example** "Managed Properties. Better Living." in white 40 px Montserrat 700 on a 70 percent Green overlay across the
lower half of the image.

**Bend when** The image is naturally dark and quiet where the text sits: no overlay needed if contrast measures at least
4.5 to 1.

**Prior art** WCAG 1.4.3 and 1.4.11; solid scrims behind text on images are the common remedy.

### M7 · Illustration

*Strength: Avoid · Source: Proposed*

**Rules**

- **Avoid** illustration, 3D renders and mascots. The brand speaks through photography, the roof motif and icons.
  _(Board)_
- **Consider** flat diagrams built from the palette, the roof form and the line icons where a concept must be explained
  (a payment flow, a lease timeline). _(Proposed)_

**Why** The board shows no illustration; adding a new visual language would break Consistency (F4).

**Example** A "how it works" strip: three line icons joined by a gold rule, with a short sentence under each.

**Bend when** A campaign commissions illustration: brief it to the palette and flat style, and record it as a decision.

**Prior art** Restrained systems such as GOV.UK avoid decorative illustration; Material uses it sparingly.
